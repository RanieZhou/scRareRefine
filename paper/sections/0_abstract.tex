Semi-supervised single-cell annotation tools such as scANVI learn batch-corrected latent representations and jointly incorporate partial labels, yet systematically under-recover rare cell populations: when fewer than 20 labeled rare cells are available in training, the softmax classifier is dominated by majority-class examples, producing near-random predictions even when the rare type is geometrically well-separated in latent space. We present \textbf{scRareRefine}, a post-hoc inductive rescue framework that augments scANVI predictions without retraining. The pipeline introduces the \emph{separability ratio} $S = \delta_r / \rho_r$---the ratio of the rare prototype's distance to the nearest majority prototype over the intra-class radius of the rare training cluster---as a pre-deployment diagnostic. When $S > 1.3$, a prototype-distance gate flags likely misassigned cells, and a training-set marker-gene signature confirms biological plausibility; when $S < 1.1$, predictions fall back to scANVI without modification.

Evaluated across seven datasets representing nine rare cell types spanning immune, pancreatic, and endothelial lineages, scRareRefine achieves average F1 gains of $+38.4$ percentage points on the four rare types with $S > 1.3$ and scANVI baseline F1 below 0.75, while producing near-zero modification ($|\Delta\text{F1}| < 0.02$) on five cell types with lower separability or well-calibrated scANVI predictions. The strongest result is cDC1 annotation with only five labeled rare-class training cells: scRareRefine achieves F1 = 0.986 versus F1 = 0.003 for scANVI and F1 = 0.000 for $k$-nearest-neighbour classification. The separability ratio---computable from training data alone, before any query inference---reliably separates cases where rescue improves recall from cases where abstention is correct, making it a practical guide for deploying prototype-based rescue in production annotation pipelines.
