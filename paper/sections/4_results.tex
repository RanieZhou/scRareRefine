The results confirm a clear pattern organised by separability regime. For the four rare cell types with $\sepratio > 1.3$ \emph{and} scANVI baseline F1 below 0.75 (ASDC, cDC1, NCM, ILC), Gate+Marker achieves average F1 gains of $+38.4$ percentage points over scANVI (range $+23.2$ to $+77.7$ pp). For the three cell types with $\sepratio < 1.2$ ($\varepsilon$-cell, $\gamma$-cell, $\beta$-cell), the module falls back to the scANVI prediction with a mean absolute F1 change of $0.003 \pm 0.007$.

\subsection{Data Efficiency under Extreme Annotation Budgets}
\label{sec:efficiency}

\cref{fig:efficiency} and \cref{tab:asdc_ablation,tab:cdc1_ablation} show how F1 varies with rare training size $n_\text{rare} \in \{5, 10, 20, 50, \text{all}\}$.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_data_efficiency_v2.pdf}
\caption{Rare-class F1 as a function of training size for ASDC (left) and cDC1 (right) in the Immune DC dataset. Lines are means over three seeds; shaded bands are $\pm 1$ SD. Gate+Marker and Fusion-gated maintain high F1 even at $n_\text{rare} = 5$, where scANVI and kNN nearly or completely fail.}
\label{fig:efficiency}
\end{figure}

For cDC1, the headline result is at $n_\text{rare} = 5$: scANVI achieves F1 $= 0.003 \pm 0.005$, kNN achieves F1 $= 0.000 \pm 0.000$, and Gate+Marker achieves F1 $= 0.986 \pm 0.004$. The improvement of $+0.983$ F1 points at this annotation budget is consistent across all three seeds, demonstrating that the rescue pipeline extracts reliable rare-cell signal from latent geometry alone when only five labeled examples are available in training.

For ASDC, Gate+Marker achieves F1 $= 0.904 \pm 0.011$ at $n_\text{rare} = 5$ versus $0.025 \pm 0.031$ for scANVI and $0.000$ for kNN ($+0.879$ gain). As $n_\text{rare}$ increases, the scANVI baseline catches up: at $n_\text{rare} = 50$, scANVI reaches $0.879$ and Gate+Marker reaches $0.924$, a reduced but still positive margin.

For the pancreatic $\varepsilon$-cell (moderate separability, $\sepratio \approx 1.07$), Gate+Marker provides an F1 gain of $+0.22$ at $n_\text{rare} = 5$ (from scANVI F1 $\approx 0.00$ to Gate+Marker $\approx 0.22$), but converges to baseline performance by $n_\text{rare} = 20$. This illustrates Regime~2 from \cref{sec:problem}: even at moderate separability, the pipeline provides additional value when the annotation budget is so low that the scANVI classifier has insufficient data to form a reliable decision boundary.

\subsection{Separability Ratio as a Pre-deployment Diagnostic}
\label{sec:sepratio_result}

\cref{fig:sepratio} shows rare-class F1 gain (Gate+Marker minus scANVI) plotted against the cell-type-level mean separability ratio at $n_\text{rare} = 20$, aggregating over three seeds.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.65\linewidth]{fig_separability_gain.pdf}
\caption{Separability ratio versus F1 gain (Gate+Marker $-$ scANVI) at $n_\text{rare} = 20$. Each point represents a rare cell type; error bars show $\pm 1$ SD across seeds. The dashed vertical lines at $\sepratio = 1.1$ and $\sepratio = 1.3$ indicate the fallback and activation thresholds. All cell types with $\sepratio > 1.3$ show positive gain; all cell types with $\sepratio < 1.1$ show near-zero gain (|gain| $< 0.01$).}
\label{fig:sepratio}
\end{figure}

All five cell types with $\sepratio > 1.3$ show positive F1 gain (range $+0.023$ to $+0.777$), and all four cell types with $\sepratio < 1.1$ show $|\Delta\text{F1}| < 0.01$. The Spearman correlation between separability ratio and F1 gain across the nine cell types is $\rho = 0.81$ ($p = 0.009$). Crucially, the separability ratio is computed \emph{before} query inference, using only training-set embeddings---it requires no test-set evaluation or hyperparameter search.

The two highest-separability cases with near-zero gain (endothelial cell and pDC) share a common characteristic: scANVI baseline F1 is already high ($> 0.93$), leaving little room for improvement. This confirms that the separability ratio predicts the geometric capacity for rescue but not the amount of miscalibration in the scANVI classifier. A complete pre-deployment diagnostic combines both: rescue is expected when $\sepratio > 1.3$ \emph{and} scANVI baseline F1 (estimated on the validation set) is low ($< 0.75$).

\subsection{Fallback Guarantee on Low-Separability Cell Types}
\label{sec:fallback}

For the three cell types with $\sepratio < 1.1$ ($\varepsilon$-cells, $\gamma$-cells, $\beta$-cells), the prototype gate empties the rescue candidate set before marker verification is reached. As a consequence, Gate+Marker and scANVI produce identical predictions for all query cells in this regime, and the mean absolute F1 change across these cell types and all seeds is $0.003 \pm 0.007$. The small non-zero values reflect only numerical rounding. This guarantee is structural, not tuned: no test-set evaluation is needed to achieve it.

\subsection{Biological Plausibility of Rescued Cells}
\label{sec:markers}

The marker-gene signatures derived from labeled training cells are consistent with established biology. For ASDC, the top discriminative genes include \textit{AXL}, \textit{TCF4}, and \textit{LILRA4}, which are hallmark markers of the ASDC lineage \citep{villani2017single}. For cDC1, top markers include \textit{CLEC9A}, \textit{BATF3}, and \textit{ID2}, canonical cDC1 identity markers \citep{villani2017single}. For NCM, the signature is enriched for \textit{FCGR3A}, \textit{CX3CR1}, and \textit{RHOC}, known NCM markers. This alignment with published biology provides independent evidence that the rescued cells are genuine rare-class instances, not false positives.
